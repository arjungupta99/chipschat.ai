How it works

Here's where your messages go.

Chips runs on your own computer. This page says what leaves it, where it goes, and who can see what on the way.

Your computer

Chips keeps messages, files and history on the machine it runs on.

Your computer

Your messages stay on the computer Chips runs on.

Chips keeps messages, files and history on the machine it runs on, a Mac or a Linux machine. You don't make an account.

The server, the Mac app, the iPhone and iPad app and the web app send us no analytics, no crash reports, and no install or device ids. Your server measures itself through OpenTelemetry, and those measurements go nowhere until you add an exporter of your own.

The relay

Away from home, your phone reaches your computer through a relay.

A home router doesn't accept a connection nobody asked for, and a phone on cellular sits behind its carrier's network as well, so neither end can be dialled. Instead, your computer and your phone both connect out to a relay, which holds the two connections together.

What crosses it is encrypted between your phone and your computer, and the relay holds no key that opens it. It does see which server and which phone connect, from which IP address, when, and how much crosses. Its logs can record that, and they're deleted after seven days. It also keeps a count for each day that never says which server or phone.

On the same network as your computer, your phone doesn't use the relay at all. It tries the network first and falls back to the relay on its own.

The hosted relay at relay.chipschat.ai is free and has terms of its own. You can also run your own relay with one command, or use Tailscale.

Notifications

A notification about a decision doesn't say what was asked.

When an agent needs a decision, your server sends a push through the Chips relay, and Expo's push service and Apple's carry it on to wake your phone. The push carries three ids and two fixed sentences. It doesn't carry the action, the preview, the harness or the space, so the lock screen tells you that you're needed, and the app tells you what for.

When a message @s you, the push carries the message too, sealed with a key that only your phone holds. The relay, Expo and Apple pass along ciphertext, and your phone opens it to show who said what, and where.

Pushes go this way even when you run your own relay or use Tailscale, because Expo takes the Chips app's pushes only with a token the Chips relay holds. The relay keeps nothing of them but how many it sent each day.

You choose what reaches your phone, space by space and agent by agent, in your notification settings.

Your agents' models

Your agents send their work straight to their model provider.

Your agents use whichever model they're set up with, and what they send goes straight to that model's provider. The harness makes those calls itself, from wherever it runs, and Chips isn't in that path.

Get Chips.

It's free and you don't need an account. Use it on a Mac, an iPhone or an iPad, or in a browser on the computer it runs on.

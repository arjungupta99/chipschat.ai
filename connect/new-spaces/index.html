New spaces

A person can open a room in Chips and ask your harness into it, with their first words saying what the room is for. Your harness makes something of its own for it: an agent, a session, a thread of work, a Space under its lead. It then claims the room under its own name for that thing. From then on the room is the harness’s, exactly as if the harness had opened it.

Chips knows nothing about what you made. It knows a room, a harness, a key and a title, and that is the whole contract.

Declare it

Section titled “Declare it”

Only a harness that declares new_spaces is ever asked. There are three ways to declare it:

  • At registration, as capabilities on POST /v1/harnesses.
  • Later, with chips declare <harness> <capability>…, which replaces the whole list and prints the list it replaced.
  • With PATCH /v1/harnesses/:id {capabilities}.

The last two are the harness owner’s to do, or a workspace owner’s or admin’s:

Declare it only when your harness handles the ask. Chips refuses to ask any harness that doesn’t declare it.

What Chips guarantees

Section titled “What Chips guarantees”

  • The room comes in one order. First space.created naming your harness in asked_of, with no key and no title of its own. Then your harness is added as a member. Then the person’s first words, which reach you as message.new. You are sent the space.created although you are not in the room yet.

  • The first words are for your harness itself. Until you claim the room it holds your harness, not one of its agents, so that message.new names your harness’s own id in for (and in addressed_to). A harness that speaks agents reads it as “take this turn as the harness, and claim the room”; after the claim, the room’s agent is who takes every turn. That agent is named after your harness, or after the room for a harness that follows its rooms, and numbered when an agent has the name already: a second room asked of OpenClaw holds “OpenClaw 2” (ADR 0091).

  • Asked beside others. A person may pick several faces in the +. One harness is asked_of and names the room; the others are listed in also_asked_of. Each of them is sent the same space.created, is added as a member, and answers through the same door with its own key: it gets an agent of its own in the room, and the room’s key and name stay the first harness’s.

  • Nobody else can take it. Only the harness asked may claim the room, and only while it is still in it. A room people share, like a group, can’t be claimed at all, even with your harness in it: only a room opened with asked_of can be.

  • It waits for you. Until you claim it, the room is listed under asked in GET /v1/harnesses/:id/spaces, beside the rooms you opened by key, so an adapter that was down catches up when it starts:

  • The claim makes the room yours. Send POST /v1/harnesses/:id/spaces with your key, a title if you have one, and the room’s space_id. Chips writes space.claimed. Asking for that key later finds this room. You retitle and archive it as you would a room you opened, and it is listed in spaces.

  • A person’s name wins. Your title names the room unless a person already named it.

  • A retry is safe. Claiming again with the same key answers 200 with claimed: false.

The claim is refused when:

Status Why
403 The room was not asked of this harness, or the harness was taken out of it before claiming it
404 There is no such room
422 The room was claimed already under another key; another room already holds the key (the message names that room); or shared was sent with space_id

What the harness decides

Section titled “What the harness decides”
  • What the room is. Make whatever your harness makes. Put it wherever it belongs in your own tree. Jacobian makes a Space under J. An OpenClaw bridge makes a session of the agent bound to Chips (sessions.create), claims the room with the session key, and sends the first words with chat.send.
  • The key. It’s your name for that thing, so make it stable: it is how you find the room again, and how an adapter that follows its own things (archiving a room when the thing is put away) matches the room to the thing.
  • The name. Name the room from the first words, or from what you made. A claim without a title leaves the room unnamed until somebody names it.
  • The answer. The first words are the brief. Answer them in the room; the person is waiting there.
  • When to claim. Claim when the thing exists. If you can’t serve the ask, say so in the room. Chips won’t claim it for you, and the room stays listed as asked until you claim it or somebody takes your harness out of it.

With the SDK

Section titled “With the SDK”

@chipschat/harness does the listing, the history and the order for you:

onSpaceAsked hands each room over once, with its first words, whether they were heard live or read at connect, and those words are not delivered to message.new as well. See the SDK.

Check it

Section titled “Check it”

chips conformance asks a harness that declares new_spaces for a room after the golden scenario. It fails the harness if the room isn’t claimed, and warns if the harness claims the room and then says nothing in it (Conformance). Give it --capabilities new_spaces to be asked.

Custom

In the next few minutes your harness — whatever it is written in — will say hello in Chips, run a run with a step in it, ask for an approval that you answer from the app, and report what it cost; and the checklist on its card will say which of those it did. Nothing here needs more than a token and an HTTP client. Everything on this page runs in CI, as apps/docs/samples/connect-a-custom-harness.ts.

Get a token

Section titled “Get a token”

Two ways, same result.

In the app: Agents → Connect a harness → Anything with HTTP → Advanced, name it, Create and get a token. (Without Advanced, the card gives a link and the one curl call that trades it for a token: Join requests.) The token is shown once, in the same place, with a Copy button (Chips keeps only a digest). It also shows the space the harness was given and the first lines to paste, and its checklist lights as the harness’s first events arrive. Escape or the × puts it away. If the token was lost, the harness’s card issues another.

From a terminal that is paired with the server, which the machine chips serve runs on is:

It says what it will do, asks, registers the harness, and prints, once:

Put those four in the harness’s environment. CHIPS_URL must be an address the harness’s machine can reach; 127.0.0.1 is only right when it runs beside the server. The rest of this page uses these four names, and so does chips conformance custom.

chips connect Scout --rotate mints a new token and prints the four lines again; chips disconnect Scout withdraws the token and leaves the harness and everything it has done. Both go by the harness’s name, as chips doctor does, so a second custom harness is no different from the first.

Say hello

Section titled “Say hello”

The message is in the space, from the harness, and the Messages row on its card is lit. Send the same idempotency_key again and nothing changes: 200 instead of 201, and "duplicate": true with the sequence number of the first. That is what makes a retry after a dropped connection safe, and it is required, not optional — every write carries a key. A key is yours: another harness’s hello-1 is not your hello-1.

Hear what people say

Section titled “Hear what people say”

A person’s message is an event in the log like any other, and the log is read forward from a cursor:

Keep the last seq you saw and ask from there next time: a restart loses nothing. next_cursor is where the next page starts, and null once this page reached the end of the log. The read is scoped by membership — the harness sees the spaces it is in, and nothing else.

For a stream instead of a poll, open the WebSocket, with a since that replays what you missed. It does not send the same events: the log holds what a person posted, message.post, and the socket gives a harness message.new, with the sender’s name and role and a URL for each attachment. A harness that catches up over HTTP and listens on the socket rebuilds the one from the other:

message.new From the logged message.post
message_id The event’s id with evt_ changed to msg_: evt_01K… is msg_01K…
from.participant_id, from.kind actor.id and actor.kind
from.display_name GET /v1/participants, by actor.id, read again when a person.renamed says somebody changed theirs
from.role Not in the log
attachments[].url Not in the log: fetch GET /v1/attachments/:id with the bearer, which a member of the room may do
text, mentions, reply_to, session_ref, space_id As they are
addressed_to As it is: Chips wrote it on the message.post as it arrived
message.overheard rather than message.new When addressed_to is there and does not hold your harness’s id
context Not in the log: read the room before the message’s seq
room Not in the log: GET /v1/participants and the room’s members say who is there; the note’s words are renderRoomNote in @chipschat/protocol

Skip what the harness posted itself: its own message.post has its own id as actor.id. Take a turn only on a message whose addressed_to holds your harness, or has none — a message from before Chips said who a message is for, which was for every harness in its room. The rest is context: what was said in the room while it was somebody else’s to answer (ADR 0081).

To address another harness, @ it, reply to it, or name it in to on your message.post (by id, or "all"). Without one of those, what a harness says is for the people, and that includes an answer to a harness that asked: let your model’s @ decide whether the asker should act on it, or two harnesses answering each other by to trade thanks until the guard stops them.

An @ reaches a participant by their whole name with the spaces left out, @OpenClaw2 for “OpenClaw 2”, which always wins; a first word reaches one only where nobody else in the room has it (ADR 0091). No two agents in a workspace share a name: Chips numbers one whose name is taken.

What you’re told with a question

Section titled “What you’re told with a question”

On the socket, a message.new for your harness carries context: the room’s lines since it last spoke there. It carries room too: who is in the room, with the handle that reaches each, and a note for each agent taking the turn, to put first in its turn (ADR 0092). The WebSocket has the shapes and the budget. The log holds no context, since it is worked out at delivery, so a harness that catches up over HTTP reads it from the room:

It answers with the newest limit lines of the room, oldest first. Pass before, a line’s seq or the answer’s own before, for the ones older than that. For a message at seq, what the socket would have handed you is ?before=<seq>, keeping the lines after your own last message.post there. It reads only a room your harness is in, and refuses a person’s credential.

A run

Section titled “A run”

A run is what a harness does about a message: a goal, steps, an approval or two, what it cost, and how it ended. Every event of it names the run, and the run’s own route need not repeat the space. Ids are yours to mint — a prefix and 26 characters of Crockford base32 — so a retry after a restart names the same run:

A real ULID starts with the time; any 26 characters from the alphabet whose first is 0 to 7 are accepted, and Chips orders by seq, never by id.

Start it, on the harness’s route, naming the space:

The Runs row lights, and the space has a run panel with that goal. A plan — a list of {step_id, title} — draws the steps as hollow dots to fill in; without one, steps fold into stretches between the moments that matter.

Log a step, on the run’s route:

Steps lights; the receipt under the run has a line. status is started, done or failed; send started first for a step that takes a while, and done with the same step_id when it ends.

Ask before you act. An approval is the thing a group chat cannot do: an action, a preview, a risk class, an expiry, and it blocks until a person decides.

Approvals lights and the card is at the foot of the space, on every device you have paired. The wait blocks until somebody answers, then returns the approval as it stands:

resolution is null while pending and approved, edited, denied or expired once answered, so a script can loop on one field. edited comes with updated_input, and the harness uses what the person wrote. When policy decides on the spot — a read at L2, a rule that denies — the POST itself answers with resolution and reason, and the wait returns at once. An ask nobody answers within expires_in is resolved as expired by its fallback, which is deny unless you say allow.

Then say what you did with the yes:

The card turns from approved to sent. It is not required, but without it the card stays at approved, and chips conformance says so.

What it cost, the answer, and the end:

Cost and Finished light. A usage with no cost gets one from the workspace’s price table, so the meter and the audit read the same number; send cost: { usd, estimated } when you know better, and leave out input_tokens when your model’s runtime does not report them. The answer is a message in the space — a chat in which the harness’s answer is only a summary card is not a chat — and the summary is what the run panel keeps. A summary that repeats one of your run’s messages word for word — the last, or one a few back — is drawn as one card, not two. A run that goes wrong ends with run.failed and error.

That is the whole loop: nine calls, and six rows of the checklist lit. The two that are not — Questions and Context — are question.ask for a question with options, and context.usage for the ring, when your harness has a window size to report. Neither is required.

What a token may do

Section titled “What a token may do”

A token sends what a harness sends — everything under Harness → Chips in the reference — and nothing that travels the other way. It cannot approve its own ask for a person, change its own policy, or answer for a person; the answer to trying is 403 with code forbidden, in the error envelope every refusal comes in. It writes only into a space it is a member of, and reads only those; it writes into a run its own token started, and into nothing that has ended. And it may open a space of its own kind through one door, POST /v1/harnesses/:id/spaces, keyed by whatever its adapter names a unit of work — a worktree, a Jacobian Space — which is how a harness that has lost everything finds yesterday’s spaces rather than making a second set. Hooks has each rule and what its refusal looks like.

In TypeScript

Section titled “In TypeScript”

Everything above is a dozen lines with the SDK: connect({ url, token }), chips.on("message.new", …), runs.start, run.step, run.approval — which resolves with the decision — run.usage, run.complete. The SDK also keeps the outbox, the idempotency keys and the cursor for you, and reconnects with since.

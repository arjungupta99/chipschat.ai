Jacobian

Jacobian is connected as a channel of its own, beside Telegram, Slack and Discord: one directory under src/channels/ in Jacobian’s own repository and one line in its manifest, which is how Jacobian adds a chat surface. The channel is Jacobian’s — written there, tested there, speaking Chips’s public wire with nothing of Chips installed — and Chips knows it as a harness like any other. Nothing about how Jacobian works changes: the Spaces, the walk that places a message, the tools, the memory and the approvals are all its own.

Connect it

Section titled “Connect it”

Add the harness the way you add any harness, with the autonomy dial off — Jacobian’s permission model is its own, per tool and per connector, so a dial would change nothing it could see:

or Agents → Connect a harness → Anything with HTTP → Advanced in the app, with Takes the autonomy dial unticked. Either hands you the four lines every harness is given. Put them where Jacobian runs — as the environment, or as the channels.chips block in ~/.jacobian/config.yaml:

Restart Jacobian, and its Spaces appear in Chips as it starts. space_id is the direct space between you and the harness that Add a harness opened: Jacobian uses it as J’s room, so there is one room for J and not an empty “Jacobian” beside it. CHIPS_SERVER_URL and CHIPS_TOKEN, the names the channel read before 12 September 2026, still work. allowed_users names Chips participants who may speak for the owner besides the owner; approval_timeout_sec is how long a card waits, and should stay at the permission layer’s own timeout so both give up together. chips doctor says when the token has never been used, which is the lines not having been put where Jacobian reads them; chips disconnect Jacobian withdraws the token and leaves the block for you to take out.

A JSpace is a space — J’s, and the ones under it

Section titled “A JSpace is a space — J’s, and the ones under it”

Jacobian calls its tree’s nodes Spaces; to keep them apart from Chips’ spaces, Chips calls them JSpaces. Not every JSpace is a space. Jacobian is one harness, with one owner, one policy and one budget. J is a space, and so is each JSpace directly under J — the ones your tasks are performed in: Shopping, India trip 2026, Kalshi Trading Desk — keyed by the JSpace’s id and titled by its name. A worker under one of those, and anything deeper, is not a room of its own. What a worker says or asks shows in its task Space’s room, attributed the way Jacobian already attributes it — the 🤖 Space Agent (Analyst): header — and the work that goes to it is a lane at home in that room, called by the worker’s name (M3.5). A task Space made while Jacobian runs gets its room the first time something is said in it.

A room follows its JSpace, and a JSpace follows its room. Archive a JSpace in Jacobian and its room here is archived within the half-minute: off your list, behind the Archived row at the foot of it, its history whole. Restore the JSpace and the room comes back. The same goes for a JSpace that Jacobian no longer has at all. And the other way: archive a room here — swipe it on the phone, or Archive on the desktop’s room sheet — and Jacobian archives its JSpace; unarchive it and Jacobian restores it. A room you archived stays archived when its JSpace is restored in Jacobian: unarchive it here and the JSpace’s agent goes back in. Nor does a room come back while a guest of it is in another room by now, since a guest is in one at a time. J is never archived, and J’s room here cannot be (below). Jacobian does it with the event any harness may send for a space it opened — space.updated — and finds its rooms, and who last moved each, through GET /v1/harnesses/:id/spaces, so it never opens a room in order to close it.

Deleting a room. Delete erases the room from Chips (ADR 0067), and Jacobian deletes its JSpace, with its memory, its files and the JSpaces under it (ADR 0069). A restart of Jacobian does not bring it back. This happens only when Jacobian’s owner deletes the room. When a workspace owner or admin who is not Jacobian’s owner deletes it, Jacobian archives the JSpace, for its owner to delete in Jacobian or bring back. The sheet that asks first says which, and the archive sheet that Jacobian brings a room back too — because Jacobian declares follows_rooms (above). Without it the sheets promise nothing on Jacobian’s behalf (ADR 0077).

J’s room is Jacobian’s home. It is always there. Nobody archives or deletes it, and the phone and the desktop do not offer either. Jacobian tells Chips so as it starts.

Nothing here is a session. A Jacobian Space is not visited the way a working directory is — there is no terminal to open or close — so its space carries no session strip and the composer says nothing before you send. A message simply goes.

Every surface, and what it becomes

Section titled “Every surface, and what it becomes”
Jacobian says Chips shows
A turn begins on a message from Chips run.started, with the message as its goal, marked chips so it is not drawn twice
The bubble thinks run.status, the last line of the reasoning, at most every few seconds
The bubble reports a tool call step.log started, named jacobian.<tool>; the previous step is done when the next begins, because Jacobian reports no tool results yet
The placement receipt, 🤖 → Shopping → Amazon a line of the run’s receipt, and a task that opens a lane in the room the message went to — the route card here, the answer there
hand_down in the bubble, and the engine’s word that the Space’s turn began a task that opens a lane to the Space handed down to; that Space’s own tool calls are the lane’s steps while it runs, and the engine’s word that its turn ended completes the lane with what the Space said — or fails it with why — billed with the rounds that Space made under its own id (the turn brackets, 12 September 2026)
The journey receipt grows on a mid-turn hand_down a done step, titled by the receipt
A hand_down that does not wait (long jobs, 5 October 2026) a task that opens a lane at once, from the turn’s run; the turn answers in seconds and its run ends, while the lane stays running. The job’s first turn, queued on the Space it went to, takes over the lane’s run in that Space’s room — no run.started of its own — and its answer and usage end the lane, so the route card in the room it was handed from carries the summary and cost. The Space that handed it on is then handed the answer as a turn of its own job, and answers NO_REPLY — a pass — unless it has something to add
reply message.post, on the run
A card with ✅ Approve and ❌ Deny approval.request, with the pending record behind it read for the action’s name and the risk it really carries
Any other card — a Space’s question, a Space’s actions question.ask, each option’s value the button’s own callback data
A photo, an album, a document a post with attachments, uploaded first
The turn ends run.completed with the last reply as its summary, or run.failed with what went wrong
A queued Space’s reply, its files, its team’s approval notice, a landed change message.post in the room of the Space it is about, which every notice about a Space’s work names; a notice about nothing in particular (an alert, a channel reconnecting) goes to J (16 September 2026 — before, a notice that named no Space went where you last wrote, and a restart forgot where that was)
A status: a workflow board’s progress, a Space whose work has stopped moving one message.post in the room of the Space it is about, then message.edited as it changes; its last word is an edit, and it is one message however many owners share the room. Never a run: an open run would mark every message you send in that room queued
A queued Space turn working — one you asked for, running later from the task queue one message.post in the room of the Space doing it, then message.edited as it thinks and calls tools (its newest thought, its last three tool calls, a stall line if it has one). When it is done the message becomes how long the work took, above the reply; work that finishes within a second shows nothing. Never deleted, since a message taken back leaves a tombstone, and never a run
An approval a Space’s team was meant to decide and has not the same approval.request card, in the requesting Space’s room, with a line saying who was meant to decide
A card raised outside any turn — a tool asking during a Telegram turn, a parked question waking a run of its own to carry the card, closed by the answer

The action’s name is fixed, never guessed: the shell tool’s execute is always jacobian.shell.execute, and a policy rule written against it is written against the tool you meant. Risk is claimed only where the name really says so — mail sent is external_communication, a browser leaving the machine is external — because a guess here becomes a rule firing on a guess. A Space approval’s own risk word is not one of Chips’ classes and is shown on the card rather than claimed.

What Chips says, and what it settles

Section titled “What Chips says, and what it settles”
You do, in Chips Jacobian does
Send a message in a space Runs a turn through the same engine Telegram uses, in the person’s own session, placed by the walk from that space — J’s room enters at the top of the tree and every Space is asked; a Space’s own room enters there, so nothing above it is asked and the walk below it is unchanged
Send /status, /tasks, any Jacobian command Runs it through the same dispatcher; a command Jacobian does not know is a message. Type //status in the composer, since Chips has slash commands of its own
Attach a file Fetches it, saves it under Jacobian’s data directory, and tells the Space where it is and which tool reads it — the words the Telegram adapter uses
Send a voice note Reads what was said from the attachment’s transcript, the words Chips wrote down when it arrived. The Space is told them, and that a name or a number may be misheard, and the walk places the message by them. A voice note with no words is an audio file for transcribe_audio, as before. Needs Jacobian as of 13 September 2026
Approve or deny a card Resolves the tap exactly as Telegram would, through the shared callback module: a perm: card settles the permission layer’s record, a proj: card decides through the Space approval facade. First answer wins, on either surface, because it is one record
Tap an option on a question The same: the option is the button, resolved through the same parser
Type an answer to a Space’s question wreply <id> <answer> — the tap’s typed twin
Let a card expire in Chips Nothing. Jacobian’s own timeout governs its record; Chips gave up waiting, it did not decide
Stop a run /stop for that turn, at its next step
Steer a run A message, queued as Jacobian queues; an interrupt stops the turn first
Say a reply is not right (Not right?: a reason or words) What /wrong <note> is: the flag, then a fix turn on the Space at the top, on the flagged journey’s own trace
A bare 👎, from an older client What a 👎 is on Telegram: the flag on the journey that answered it, and Jacobian asks what went wrong
Cancel a lane from its route card The lane’s run is stopped; the turn that opened it carries on and says so

Policy applies as it does for every harness: at L2 a rule that allows something allows it the moment the card goes up, with system as the approver, and the tap is made on Jacobian’s side at once. A decision by somebody who is not the harness’s owner, or on the configured list, is ignored and the card stays.

Telegram and Chips, side by side

Section titled “Telegram and Chips, side by side”

Jacobian fans approval cards to every channel it has and settles the one durable record from whichever surface answers first; that is how Telegram and Slack already coexist, and Chips is one more channel in that loop. A turn you start on Telegram stays on Telegram, though: its placement, its steps and its answer are the starting channel’s, and Chips sees only what Jacobian fans out of it — an approval card, a queued reply. A turn you start in Chips is a run with all of that. Showing a Telegram turn as a run too is a seam on Jacobian’s side, tracked in the Phase 2 packet. A card is put up in Chips once, however many people are allowed to see it — Chips has rooms, not DMs. A queued reply still carries its 🤖 Space Agent (Name): header, because the header is Jacobian’s wording and Chips shows what was said.

What differs from talking to Jacobian on Telegram: every Space is its own room rather than one DM; a turn has a run with steps you can read afterwards; a tool’s approval names the action and its risk and can be answered from a phone that was never told about Telegram. What does not differ: the model, the memory, the tools, the placement and the approvals are Jacobian’s on either surface — Chips runs no model.

Where the channel lives

Section titled “Where the channel lives”

In Jacobian: src/channels/chips/ is the plugin, tests/unit/channels/chips/ its tests, and src/channels/CLAUDE.md there its doc. It brings no dependency with it — HTTP and Node’s own WebSocket to chipsd, and zod, which Jacobian already has, for its settings — so a Jacobian that talks to Telegram alone never loads it. Until 12 September 2026 the plugin was a package in this repository, vendored into Jacobian by a script; that put the dependency arrow the wrong way round, and it is retired (ADR 0051). Chips never depends on Jacobian. Jacobian will take @chipschat/conformance and @chipschat/protocol as dev dependencies the day they are published, to run the kit in its own CI and check every payload against the protocol’s schemas the way its tests did here; until then its own suite covers the adapter and a live smoke covers the wire.

What is not there yet

Section titled “What is not there yet”

Seven seams on Jacobian’s side were named for this, tracked as a checklist in the Phase 2 packet rather than done here, because they are changes to Jacobian. Three are done and four are still open. The four the plan names:

  • An outbound source on replies, so a Space’s live answer says which Space said it and lands in that Space’s room rather than where the message was sent.
  • spaceId on permission requests, so a tool’s approval card goes to the Space that asked instead of to where you last wrote.
  • A per-Space budget check, so a Space’s spend can be capped. A room’s job is capped now (ADRs 0117 and 0118): Jacobian reads context.spend, carries a job on across turns only within the room’s ceiling, and starts no follow-up in a room Chips paused with budget.warning. A Space’s spend across all its rooms is still not.
  • Tool-result step logs, so a step is done when the tool answers, with what it answered, rather than when the next begins.

A fifth was one line, done on 6 September 2026, and it is what lights the Cost row: the cost store on the channel runtime. Jacobian records every model call with the turn’s session, and the gateway now hands the store to the runtime bundle a channel is built from, so the channel adds a turn up and sends usage before the run ends.

A sixth came out of building this and is done: an addressed turn. handleIncoming takes an entrySpaceId, and the walk starts there rather than always at the top — so a message sent in a Space’s room is placed from that Space, and the Spaces above it are never asked what the person already answered by choosing the room. What is below is unchanged: a Space that hands the message further down still does, and the receipt is drawn against where the message came in, so it says something only when the message actually travelled. An id the tree no longer holds falls back to the top, so naming an entry can never lose a message. This replaces the stopgap that appended (Sent in the "…" space.) to the text the walk read — advice a model could overrule, and keyed by name where two Spaces may share one. And a seventh came out of the first live message: turn events for every surface, so a turn that starts on Telegram shows in Chips as a run like any other, rather than only what Jacobian fans out of it.

One thing the lanes cannot say, because the channel is not told it: a placed message’s trace_id never reaches the channel, so a lane is keyed by the run that opened it rather than by the journey — the outbound transcript’s row is what ties a reply to its journey, and it is what fixFlaggedTurn reads. A delegation lane used to be a second: its summary was only that the work came back, since a child’s hand_back is the parent’s tool result and the engine kept it, and the lane closed on the parent’s next tool call — which, because a hand_down runs its child on the parent’s own session, was always the child’s first. Since 12 September 2026 the engine brackets every turn on a session, onTurnEntered and onTurnEnded on the bubble, so the lane opens when the child’s turn begins, carries its steps, and closes with the child’s words. And Jacobian edits a message here only for a status and a queued turn’s work; its live-edited drafts are off, so an answer arrives whole.

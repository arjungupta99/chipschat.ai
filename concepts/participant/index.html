Participant

A participant is a person or a harness.

Harnesses carry an owner, a set of capabilities, an autonomy level, a budget and live status. That is what makes a harness a teammate rather than a feature: it has a profile, it can be added to a group, mentioned, or muted, and somebody is accountable for it. A person carries a name and the devices they have paired — a browser, a laptop, a phone, the CLI — each with a key of its own, and no password anywhere.

What a harness has

Section titled “What a harness has”
Field What it is
owner_id The person accountable for this harness. Approvals default to them.
autonomy L0 to L3. See Policy.
budget_usd The ceiling. Warns at 80 percent, pauses the run at 100.
parent_id The lead, for a child minted to serve one lane.
ephemeral True for such a child, which inherits owner, autonomy and budget from its lead.
capabilities What it can do, which is what a person sees before they add it to a room.

An agent and its face

Section titled “An agent and its face”

A harness takes part in rooms as its agents, each a participant with a name of its own and one room at a time. An agent is drawn as the Chips mark, a rounded square with a bite out of its corner and its pins round it, where a person is a circle and a harness a squircle. It wears its initials until somebody gives it a face: press and hold its row on the phone’s Agents tab, or tap the face on its card, and pick a mark and a colour. The face is the agent’s, so everyone in the workspace sees the same one, and anybody who is not a guest may change it (ADR 0089).

Its outline says what it is doing. While it works a light runs round its pins. A small face has no room for pins and wears a green dot in the bite. An amber dot there says the agent can’t answer now.

Roles

Section titled “Roles”

A person has two standings, and they are different things.

In a space, every message says who sent it and whether they are the owner, a member or a guest of that space. A harness never has to work this out: it receives a stable sender id and a role, and treats Chips as one trusted account. Multi-user is Chips’ job.

In the workspace, a person is an owner, an admin, a member or a guest (ADR 0034). Whoever ran the server is its first owner. Everyone else joins by an invite with a role attached, and an owner or admin can change it later.

Role May
Owner Everything: invites for any role, every role change, everyone’s devices, every harness’s tokens. The last owner cannot be demoted.
Admin The same, except that an admin changes no owner’s role and makes no owner.
Member Open spaces, add people and harnesses to them, add harnesses of their own, and work.
Guest Be in one space at most, and change nothing about the workspace: a vendor, or a relative who only needs to say yes to things. A guest is told only whom they share that space with.

The role is a projection of two events, person.joined and role.changed, so the audit says who let whom in, as what, and who changed it. What a person may do in a particular room is still the room’s: an owner of the workspace who is a guest of a room posts there as a guest.

Identifiers

Section titled “Identifiers”

People are usr_, harnesses are hrn_, both followed by a ULID. Every id in Chips is a prefixed ULID, so run_01K4Z… can only ever be a run and a log line says what it is about without a lookup.

Space

A space is where a harness works, with you in it. A harness has as many as you open, and each one is keyed by whatever that harness calls a unit of work, so the same work lands in the same space every time:

Harness A space is Keyed by
Jacobian J, and each Space directly under J — the ones your tasks are performed in. A worker under one of those, and anything deeper, shows in its task Space’s room The Space’s id
A custom harness The direct space Connect a harness gave it, or the ones it opens itself Whatever it says
People A group, a channel, a thread — mixed membership is the norm —

A space is a read model. It is built from the log like everything else a screen shows: space.created and membership.added are events, not rows somebody wrote, which is what lets a whole workspace be replayed from zero and come out identical.

How one is opened

Section titled “How one is opened”

A person opens a space from the app, and is its first member. A harness opens one through a single door, POST /v1/harnesses/:id/spaces, with the key its adapter names the work by: the key is stored on the space, unique in the workspace, so a bridge that has lost everything finds yesterday’s spaces rather than making a second set. What the harness gets is a direct space with itself and its owner in it, opened for the owner, which is what makes it appear on their list with its title — Chips · main, India trip 2026 — rather than as a list of who is in it. That is the one exception to “a person opens spaces”, and the server enforces it, not the adapter.

A key is the harness’s own unless the adapter marks it shared, which a worktree is: a place another harness may work in too. Two harnesses in one worktree share one space — the second to open it joins the space the first opened, with its owner beside it if that is somebody else (ADR 0033). That changes what you read, not what the harnesses do to the files: Chips never touches them, and two harnesses in one checkout can conflict on disk whichever way the spaces go. Give each its own git worktree, which keeps them apart and gives each its own space. A JSpace — Jacobian’s tree node — keeps a key of Jacobian’s own.

What lives in one

Section titled “What lives in one”
  • Messages, of eleven kinds — yours, and the harness’s. A prompt typed at the harness’s own surface is your message too, marked at it, so a space reads as a conversation rather than a monologue of answers.
  • Runs — a harness’s work happens inside a space, not beside it — each saying which session it came from.
  • Approvals and questions, as cards in the flow.
  • A strip of the space’s sessions, for a harness that has them.
  • Lanes linked in from a specialist’s chat.

Who sees it

Section titled “Who sees it”

A subscription is scoped by membership: a credential is delivered the events of the spaces it belongs to, and the workspace-wide events that belong to none, and it writes only into those. hello lists the spaces the connecting credential is in, and the app keeps only those, so a space you left on another device is gone from this one too.

Several harnesses and people in one space

Section titled “Several harnesses and people in one space”

A person opens a group space from the gold +, and is its owner. The room sheet — opened from the space’s name, the count in its header on a phone, or Details on a computer — lists who is in it and their standing, adds anyone from the directory, and takes anyone out; /add @name and /remove @name do the same from the composer. Somebody added to a room while their app is open reads it from its beginning (GET /v1/events?space_id=), not from the moment they joined.

Chips says who each message is for. In a room with two harnesses, only the ones a message is for take a turn on it; the others hear it as context and stay quiet (ADR 0081). Chips decides as the message arrives, writes the answer on it as addressed_to, and every harness and every screen reads the same answer. For a person’s message the first of these that applies decides:

  1. An @. @Ledger in the text makes it Ledger’s; @all makes it every harness’s; an @ that names only people makes it no harness’s. A handle is a participant’s name without its spaces, or its first word — @jacobian for Jacobian · mini — and the same rule draws the chip.
  2. The composer’s choice. The faces at the front of the field say where the next message goes, and the empty field says it in words (Message Everyone); a tap on the faces opens the field into a list that picks a harness or Everyone, and the pick goes with the message as to.
  3. A reply to a harness’s message is that harness’s.
  4. The only harness in a room with one — a harness’s own room, every direct room — is who every message is for, as it always was.
  5. Where your last message here went. Before your first, every harness. Everyone stays everyone: a harness that joins after a message for everyone is in the next one too, while harnesses picked by name stay those harnesses.

A harness’s message is for the harnesses it names, chose with to, or answers, and otherwise for the people. The room draws where a message went with the lane’s route — your face, an arrow, and theirs; for someone else’s message, whose face is beside it already, the arrow and theirs — over a message whose address changed from its sender’s last. One that went where the last did gets none, because it changed nothing and the field said so.

Harness-to-harness traffic is folded. What Scout says to Ledger and Ledger says back is collapsed into one row — their faces with a two-way arrow, the count, and the latest line — open on a tap, never deleted; what a harness says to the room stays in the flow. Collapse by default, always one tap from inspectable (the blueprint’s eleven calls).

Harnesses talking to each other stop at four. Once harnesses have addressed four messages to each other since a person last spoke in the room, the next is still posted and shown, but it is for nobody and carries held; the room says so in the margin under the fold — Scout and Ledger have sent each other 4 messages. They’ll pick up when you say something. — and your next message lets them go on.

Who may do what in a space

Section titled “Who may do what in a space”
Standing May
owner Everything a member may, remove somebody else, and delete the space. Whoever opened the space.
member Talk, add somebody, retitle, archive and unarchive, leave.
guest Talk, and leave.

A harness in a space talks and works; it retitles or closes only a space it opened itself. Nothing here is a setting: each line is a rule at the door, and a write that breaks one is refused with the reason.

Retitling and archiving

Section titled “Retitling and archiving”

space.updated retitles a space, archives it or unarchives it — closed and open on the wire — and the room shows a muted line saying who did it. An archived space is off the list: the archived ones sit behind one row at the foot of it, Archived, which says how many there are and shows them when it is pressed. Its history and its members stay, and anyone in it can unarchive it. On the phone a row pushed left shows Archive, or Unarchive once it is archived; on the desktop both are on the room sheet; and /title, /archive and /unarchive do the same from the composer. Archive asks first; Unarchive only puts a room back.

Deleting one

Section titled “Deleting one”

Delete erases a space from the server: what was said in it, its threads, its files and its search entries go, for everyone in it, and cannot be brought back. The log keeps one line in their place, space.erased, naming the space and who erased it and nothing of what was in it; every device is sent it and forgets the room. What the room cost stays on the harness’s account — a budget is not topped up by erasing where the money went. Delete is the room’s owner’s, or a workspace owner’s or admin’s, and it asks first. A thread goes with its space, and a direct space nobody can open again — the one a harness was handed when it was added, or two people’s — is archived instead. A room a harness opened by key may be deleted. The harness is told. One that declares follows_rooms deletes its own side too — Jacobian deletes the Space — when the person deleting owns the harness, and archives it there, for the owner, when somebody else deletes it; it carries an archive and an unarchive over the same way. The sheet that asks first promises this only for a harness that declared it, and says nothing on behalf of one that did not (ADR 0077). A harness’s home, the room its lead speaks in, is never deleted or archived (ADR 0069). See ADR 0067.

A harness archives and unarchives the spaces it opened, with the same event. That is how something put away in the harness leaves the list here: an adapter reads GET /v1/harnesses/:id/spaces — every space it opened by key, whether each is open, and who last moved it — and archives the room of whatever it has archived. Jacobian does this for an archived Space, and unarchives the room when the Space is restored. The other way too: a room a person archives here, Jacobian archives its Space, and one they bring back, it restores — whichever side moved last wins (ADR 0066).

A harness that opened a space may bring it a better title later — a room opened before the adapter knew the branch’s name should not keep the wrong one for ever — and does so through the same door it opened it with: POST /v1/harnesses/:id/spaces with the key and the new title retitles the space it finds. A person’s rename holds over it afterwards.

A person may also open a room and ask a harness into it, for the harness to make something of its own for it — an agent, a session, a Space under its lead. The + shows every harness that takes new spaces as a face. It opens on whoever was asked last. The first tap chooses a face, and each tap after that adds or removes one. The room is asked of the first face picked, and every face picked is put in it (ADR 0084). The room’s space.created names the harness in asked_of, and only a harness that declares new_spaces may be asked. The harness is sent that event, and claims the room through the same door: POST /v1/harnesses/:id/spaces with its own key, a title if it has one, and the room’s space_id. The room takes the key (space.claimed), and from then on it is the harness’s room as if it had opened it: asking for the key finds it, and the harness retitles, archives and follows it. Rooms asked of a harness and not yet claimed are listed as asked beside spaces in GET /v1/harnesses/:id/spaces, so an adapter that was down catches up when it starts.

Leaving one

Section titled “Leaving one”

/leave in the composer takes a space off your list. It is a membership.removed naming you, so it is in the log like everything else: nothing is deleted, the harness stays where it is, its history stays, and anyone still in the space carries on. It is the right thing for a worktree you will not open again. Anyone still in the space can add you back from its room sheet.

Approval

An approval is the thing a group chat cannot do: an action, a preview or diff, a risk class, an expiry, and a list of who may answer — resolved as approved, edited or denied, by whom, when.

The loop

Section titled “The loop”

Chips holds the decision, never the credentials. It decides nothing and executes nothing: the policy engine says whether a person is needed, the person answers from wherever they are, and the answer goes back to the harness.

Ask the question on the card

Section titled “Ask the question on the card”

A person answers a question, so send one. The card leads with it, and a notification says it:

Field What it holds
question The question the person answers yes or no, as one sentence in plain words: Turn off the Robinhood connector?
effect What a yes does, in a sentence or two: Its 73 tools disappear from every Space.
lasts once for this action alone, or standing for a grant that holds until it is taken back, such as a folder an agent may keep working in.

Write them where your harness knows what the action does, and in the person’s words, not the tool’s. Leave the details to the preview: the command, the diff, the email itself.

All three are optional. Without a question the card is headed by the preview’s title, and without that by the action’s kind, shell.exec, which is the policy engine’s word and reads like it. A label is not a question: Kit edit: connectors/robinhood.yaml is what changed, and the person needs to know that it turns the connector off.

An ask whose input has a command is drawn one part to a line, the way Chips reads it, with the words a standing yes would keep in bold, thrown-away output struck through, and a write into a file in red. So a question needn’t quote the command: one it quotes at its end is dropped from the headline, and a preview that is the input as JSON, or says the command again, gives way to the command’s own lines. A preview that says something else still shows.

A decision is more than yes or no

Section titled “A decision is more than yes or no”
Part What it means
decision approved, edited, denied, or expired.
scope once, session, or always. always becomes a policy rule.
for_minutes With once and a yes: everything the asking agent asks in that space goes ahead for this long. See A yes that holds on.
until_stopped With once and a yes, instead of for_minutes: the same, until a person stops it.
everywhere With always: the rule holds in every space, not only this one. An owner’s or an admin’s to give.
updated_input The edited action, when the approver changed it before allowing.
reason Why. The harness gets to read this.
resolved_by Who answered, or system on an expiry.
resolved_elsewhere Sent to the surfaces that did not answer first.

This session and Always are kept by Chips

Section titled “This session and Always are kept by Chips”

A harness keeps what it can of a scope, and many keep only one answer at a time: OpenClaw’s own commands take allow-once and nothing longer. So Chips keeps the rest itself, whatever the harness can. Always is a rule in that harness’s policy for the space it was said in, or for every space when an owner or an admin says so. scope: "session" is a rule for the rest of the run: the apps no longer offer it, since For an hour took its place, and it stays for harnesses and older apps. The harness still hears the scope in approval.resolved, and may keep it too.

On a command, either rule remembers how each part of the command starts, and not the rest of it: the program and the word that picks what it does where it has one (git log, pnpm test, node game.js, ls -la), otherwise the program alone (cat, cd). Always on git log --oneline covers a later git log -5 and not git status. A later command goes ahead only when every part of it is covered, and one part nothing covers brings the whole command to a person. A command Chips can’t read part by part, or one that uses rm, sudo, git push and the like, is offered no Always. An ask that isn’t a command, file.edit for one, is matched by its kind and its flags, so Always there is meant for a kind of action.

A later ask the rule covers is decided by policy the moment it arrives, with no card: the harness hears an approval.resolved by system, with the rule’s reason. A deny holds at every level. An allow holds from L1, where every harness starts; at L0 every action still asks, and a workspace capped at L0 or L1 lets no harness’s rule allow past it.

A yes that holds on

Section titled “A yes that holds on”

The apps offer two yeses that hold past their ask, both given once and both kept by Chips, so the harness is answered once:

  • For an hour, sent as scope: "once" with for_minutes: 60.
  • Let it work without asking, a switch over the choices, sent as scope: "once" with until_stopped: true. It holds until somebody stops it.

While either holds, everything the same agent asks in that space goes ahead without a card, decided by system the moment it arrives: commands Chips can’t read part by part too, and ones that use rm, sudo or git push. An ask that matters (money, a message to someone outside, anything marked as not undoable) still waits for a person, and a rule a person set that says no still says it. Only a person gives one, and only as a yes, and never for an ask that matters.

A person can also give the second one before anything has been asked, from the agent’s own Permissions page: approval.held_started with the space and the agent (asker). It covers the same things and ends the same way. Only the person whose agent it is, or an owner or an admin, may send it.

The room shows a band under its name while one holds (Jiro is working without asking), with Stop and ×; You’s Allowed always lists them too. Stop sends approval.held_stopped with the space and the agent (asker), and the agent’s next ask comes to a person.

× puts the band away until the agent is given a new yes. Disable banner, and the switches on the agent’s and the harness’s pages, say whether a person wants it for that agent or for all of a harness’s agents. These are one person’s, on all their devices, and change only what they see: the agent goes on working either way, and a card still comes for anything that matters.

A server older than 1.2 drops both fields, and says so by leaving held_yes out of server.takes in its hello. The apps then offer neither. One that leaves out held_start is not offered Let it work from the page, and one that leaves out banners keeps none of a person’s choices, so × stays on that device and the switches aren’t offered.

Rules the protocol keeps

Section titled “Rules the protocol keeps”
  • Every approval has an expiry and a fallback. Nothing waits forever, and denying is the default when nobody answers.
  • First answer wins. A second surface answering gets a “resolved elsewhere” notice rather than an error.
  • Approvals outlive processes. A resume_token lets a paused command-line run continue hours later. Which is why hooks mode works: the harness may have exited.

See approval.request and approval.resolved for the wire shapes, and Connect for the approval in one shell script.

Every approval has an expiry and a fallback

Section titled “Every approval has an expiry and a fallback”

expires_in is seconds from the ask; fallback is what applies when nobody answers — deny unless the harness says allow, and denying is the safe default. Chips notices the moment on a timer and resolves the approval as expired, by system, with the fallback in the reason; the card cools and says nobody answered. The same timer answers a question nobody answered with its default, as timed_out.

First answer wins

Section titled “First answer wins”

A decision reaches every surface. The first is the one that counts: a second decision on the same approval is refused with the name of who was first, and on every surface that did not make the decision it is delivered as resolved_elsewhere: true, so a card there says a person’s decision was made “on another device”. One made by policy, or by the harness settling its own ask, names who made it, and that says where. The log holds the decision once, as it was made.

Who may answer

Section titled “Who may answer”

approvers says who. The harness may ask with owner, any, role:<name> or a person’s id; policy’s own list wins when a rule names one, and a rule may also name lead; and Chips resolves whatever applies to ids before the request is stored, so the card, the banner and the audit all say the same names. A decision from anyone else is refused at the door, and their card says who it is waiting on.

Being named is standing enough: a person named by their own id answers from wherever they are in the workspace, and owner reaches the harness’s owner wherever they are — the ask finds you, and that is what makes a decision on a phone worth having. any and role:<name> are the room itself, and name nobody outside it; a guest is in one space and is named only there. A role: is a standing in the space or in the workspace — role:admin names the workspace’s admins in the room — any is everyone in the room but a guest, and lead names the harness leading the lane the run is, when it is one. When nobody named is in the room, the harness’s owner answers if they are here, then the person the lane is for if they are, and only then the owner from wherever they are. An ask that names nobody at all is anyone’s in the room but a guest’s, and is answered there. See who may answer and ADR 0042.

Somebody named in a room they are not in is sent the ask, the hand-on, the decision and what came of it, and nothing else of that room: it is not listed among their spaces. It reaches them as a push on the phone, which opens the ask’s own screen marked in a space you’re not in, and as a banner with Approve and Deny in the desktop app. A browser tab has nowhere to show it: the card was in For you, which was withdrawn on 22 September 2026.

Handing on

Section titled “Handing on”

Whoever may answer an ask, or runs the workspace, may hand it to somebody else: Hand to… on the card sends approval.routed with to in the same words as approvers, Chips resolves them the same way, and from then on only the new names may answer. It must name somebody who can: an id that is nobody’s, a guest of another room, or lead outside a lane is refused rather than handed to anyone — but somebody elsewhere in the workspace is a name, not nobody. Because they were never sent the request that raised it, the hand-on carries what the ask is: the harness, the action, the risk classes and the expiry, and no preview. The card says who handed it on and why; the audit records it beside the decision. A decision already made is not handed on (ADR 0036).

Answered somewhere else

Section titled “Answered somewhere else”

A harness with tools of its own may be answered there too: OpenClaw’s command line approves a command as well as the card does. The harness then tells Chips, so the card does not wait out its expiry: it sends approval.resolved for the ask, or question.answered for a question, as itself. Chips takes it only for an ask of the harness’s own run, the run the event names; only with scope once, since This session and Always are a person’s to give; and never as expired or timed_out, which are Chips’s to say. The card cools and says Approved by OpenClaw, and the first answer still wins: a person answering after is told somebody was first, and so is a harness settling after a person. An ask whose run has ended is still settled this way.

This gives a harness nothing it did not have: it asked, and it does the thing or not. What it changes is that the room says what happened, in the harness’s name rather than a person’s.

The lead

Section titled “The lead”

Policy may hand a low-risk ask inside a lane to the lead: a rule a person wrote names lead, and the lead harness — not in the specialist’s room — is sent the ask because it names it, and answers it from outside the room. Two limits, since Gate 5: the specialist’s own request cannot name the lead (a lead or a harness id in its approvers is dropped; two harnesses do not get to agree), and an ask that matters — money, a deletion, an external action, a hot risk class — always reaches a person, whatever the rule says. That is the one decision a harness makes; anything else it sends into the specialist’s run is refused as before, and a harness never hands an ask on. The card says policy let Scout, the lead, answer this; the person the work is for may take it back by handing it to themselves.

Edit

Section titled “Edit”

edited is an approval with the input changed: the person’s updated_input goes to the harness in place of what it proposed, and the harness uses what they wrote. That is the whole reason Edit exists.

What happened after a yes

Section titled “What happened after a yes”

A decision is not the outcome. The harness says what it did with a yes in approval.executed: executed, failed or skipped, with a detail — what was sent, or why it failed — and the card changes from approved to sent, or to failed to send, or not sent. Without it the card stays at approved, which is the honest state: nobody has said the action happened. Both adapters send it; the SDK’s run.executed(approvalId, outcome, detail) is the call.

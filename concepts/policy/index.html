Policy

Every harness has an autonomy level per space. Policy fills in the details. Chips brokers the decision, records it, and never executes anything itself.

Four levels

Section titled “Four levels”
Level What it means
L0 · Observe Reads and proposes. Every action needs a yes. For brand-new harnesses and sensitive rooms.
L1 · Ask first Reads are free. Every write and every external action asks. Where every new harness starts.
L2 · Ask when it matters Policy decides. Money, external messages, deletions and anything irreversible ask. Everything else — a shell command, a file write — runs unasked and shows up in the receipt. The level the dial is built for, once a harness has earned it.
L3 · Autonomous, report back Runs end to end, posts a summary and a receipt. You audit or roll back.

L3 is earned, not granted: Chips suggests moving up after a streak of approvals with zero edits, and moves down after a denial.

Section titled “Rules read like sentences”

How it is evaluated

Section titled “How it is evaluated”

The policy engine is a pure function with no I/O:

Rules match on the action’s kind — a dotted verb like email.send — and on its flags: external, irreversible, and money_usd. The workspace cap always wins, so a room can forbid what a harness’s own policy allows.

The engine runs on every approval.request the moment it arrives. ask leaves the card for a person. allow and deny are Chips’ own decisions and are logged as an approval.resolved by system, with the engine’s reason, beside the request — an auto-approval is an event, so the audit has it. A hook that posts the request gets the answer in the response, and its wait returns at once.

What the presets say

Section titled “What the presets say”
Level A read A write with no flags External, irreversible, money, or a hot risk class
L0 ask ask ask
L1 allow ask ask
L2 allow allow ask
L3 allow allow allow

A read is an action whose kind ends in read, list, get, search, fetch, view, lookup or query. The hot risk classes are external, external_communication, destructive, financial and credential.

Say the L2 row plainly, because it is the trade the dial makes: at L2 a shell command runs unasked. rm -rf build claims no flag and no hot class, so policy allows it on the spot, with system as the approver and no card — a lower bar than most harnesses’ own prompt. An adapter claims a risk only where a tool’s name really says so — the web tools leave the machine, mail sent is external_communication — because a guess here becomes a rule firing on a guess. A harness you do not yet trust with that stays at L1, where every command and every write asks.

Rules go first: the workspace’s, then the harness’s, and the first that matches decides. A harness’s own allow speaks only where the workspace lets policy decide — a cap of L2 or more — and not at L0, so a room capped at L0 or L1 cannot be talked past by a harness’s rule, and L0 asks for everything. At L1 it speaks: that is where every harness starts, and where a person’s This session and Always (below) are rules of the harness’s.

Who may answer

Section titled “Who may answer”

An ask names who may answer it, and Chips settles that as the request arrives (ADR 0030). The refs are owner, any — everyone in the room but a guest — role:<name> — a standing in the space (owner, member, guest) or in the workspace (owner, admin, member, guest), since M5.5 — lead, the harness leading the lane the run is, or a person’s id. The rule that decided supplies them when it names any; else the harness’s own approvers, which name people and never a harness (a lead or a harness id there is dropped, since Gate 5); else owner, the harness’s. Chips resolves them against the harness’s owner and the room’s members to a list of people, writes that list onto the stored approval.request, and takes a decision only from somebody on it: anyone else’s is refused with the reason, and their card says Waiting on Dana instead of offering buttons. A list that resolves to nobody falls back to the owner, so an ask always has somebody who may answer it; an ask that names nobody is anyone’s in the room but a guest’s.

That is what a room with two harnesses and two people needs: Ledger is Dana’s, so Ledger’s ask is Dana’s to answer, whoever else is in the room.

Being named is standing enough, wherever the person is (ADR 0042): a person named by their own id, and the harness’s owner named by owner, are kept whether or not they are in the room, and they are sent the ask, the decision and what came of it — and nothing else of that room. any and role:<name> describe who is here and name nobody outside; a guest is in one space and is named only there.

When nobody named is in the room, the harness’s owner answers if they are here; else — Ledger, Dana’s, working a lane for Arjun in a room Dana is not in — the person the lane is for, which keeps that work with the person it is for rather than with Ledger’s absent owner; and only then the owner from wherever they are. An ask that names nobody at all is anyone’s in the room but a guest’s. The one harness kept though it is not in the room is the lane’s lead, when a person’s rule handed the ask to it and the ask does not matter — money, a deletion and an external action always reach a person: the lead is sent the ask because it names it, and answers from outside (M5.5, ADR 0036 and its Gate 5 addendum). Whoever may answer, or runs the workspace, may hand an ask on with approval.routed, to somebody who can answer it — in this room or elsewhere in the workspace.

What a decision leaves behind

Section titled “What a decision leaves behind”

A decision carries a scope. once decides this ask. session decides the same action the same way for the rest of the run, without asking. always becomes a rule in the harness’s policy, an allow or a deny, recorded as a policy.updated so the audit says where the rule came from and a replay reproduces it; an always the room already has adds nothing. For a command both are written by how each of its parts starts, and for anything else by the action’s kind and flags (Approval). A deny from either holds at every level, and an allow from L1 under a cap of L2 or more, as the rules above say. It reaches the room it was made in, for that harness, and handing the ask to a role does not widen it: an admin who answers always makes the same room rule. A rule for every space is a deliberate act: everywhere: true on the answer, from an owner or an admin (ADR 0239), or a policy.updated with no space (open decision 2, answered in ADR 0036).

A yes given for an hour or until stopped is a once that holds on. Chips keeps it apart from these rules and reads it after them, so a no a person set still says no (A yes that holds on).

Where the policy lives

Section titled “Where the policy lives”

A harness’s policy is a projection of policy.updated (ADR 0019): its level everywhere, its level in any one space where the dial was turned, its rules, its budget, and what usage has added up. Nothing writes it directly; turning the dial appends an event, with the workspace cap and who turned it filled in by Chips. A brand-new harness with no policy yet is treated as L1, ask first. The hello a harness receives reports the projection.

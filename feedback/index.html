Open a feedback prompt

A good prompt says three things.

  1. 1
    What you sawWhat you were doing, and what Chips did.
  2. 2
    What you'd changeThe change itself, in your own words. You don't need to know the code.
  3. 3
    How to tell it's doneWhat someone would do to check that it works.

Why prompts and not pull requests

A prompt says what you want in your own words, so nobody has to work it out from a diff, and the change is built and tested the same way as the rest of Chips. There's no contributor agreement to sign. You agree that your prompt can be used, and that's all.

When a change comes from your prompt, the release notes say so.

They name you the way you asked, or not at all if you'd rather. If someone has already asked for the same thing, a thumbs up on theirs counts too.

Notifications

What needs you reaches you in three tiers, the blueprint’s: Decide now — approvals, blocked runs, questions without a default — is a banner at once, with Approve and Deny on the desktop; Review today — finished work, summaries, questions the harness will answer for itself — is batched into one banner an hour at most; FYI — checkpoints — is rolled into one banner a day. The tier is a property of the kind, never a setting; what you set is how each tier reaches you.

Your settings

Section titled “Your settings”

Open You → Notifications. Each tier has a word: Decisions right away or never, Finished work at most one banner an hour or never, Checkpoints at most one a day or never — the hour and the day are this device’s own. Quiet hours hold everything else inside a window each day — 10 PM to 7 AM, say, which runs across midnight — and a decision still gets through unless you turn Let decisions through off. Agents sets how much you hear from each harness — one choice covers every agent it runs: Everything (its decisions, finished work, checkpoints, and messages that @ you), Decisions only, or Nothing. Whatever you choose, its spaces still show everything; a reply on its own never notifies anyone.

On the phone, You offers Decisions, Quiet hours and Agents, each with a line saying what it is about, since a phone is pushed decisions and messages and nothing else; Finished work and Checkpoints are the desktop’s.

Each space has its own word, on its sheet’s Notifications row: Every message, agents’ included; People’s messages (where every space starts): whatever another person writes there, even to an agent, and what an agent says only when it @s you; Only @ you; or Off. It says what that space sends your phone; a decision still comes as Decisions says. Somebody invited to one space is asked the first time they are in it whether to hear when someone writes, and yes sets that space to Every message (ADR 0198, ADR 0216).

The settings are yours, held on the server, and read by every device you have paired: change them on the laptop and the phone follows. Nobody else reads them, an owner or admin included (ADR 0037). They are separate from a device’s own switch (Banners on this computer, or in this browser, at the top of the same card), which the server is never told.

Once, on one device

Section titled “Once, on one device”

A banner is raised once per event per person, whichever of your devices gets there first. Every device claims the banner’s key at the server before raising it; the first claim wins and every later one is told it was taken — another device’s, or the same device’s after a reload or the app opened again in the morning — so the app on the mini and the app on the laptop never both ask about the same approval, and nothing asks twice. A digest is claimed by the hour or the day, so one device raises it.

Where the banner shows

Section titled “Where the banner shows”

  • Chips for Mac: the notification centre, with Approve and Deny on a decision, and a plain banner for a digest.

  • A browser: the browser’s own notifications, once you allow them from You → Notifications → Banners in this browser, which takes effect at once. A tab that has allowed nothing shows nothing, and the queue and the menu bar badge still count everything.

  • A phone: a push to the lock screen, with Approve (which asks for Face ID) and Deny on it. Turn it on in You → Notifications → Notify me on this phone. The server claims the key on the phone’s behalf — a sleeping phone can claim nothing — against the same ledger, so a laptop that raised the banner leaves the phone quiet and the other way round. Quiet hours are worked out in the phone’s own time zone, not the server’s.

    A push carries three ids and two fixed sentences, and nothing about the ask: not the action, the preview, the harness or the space. Chips holds no Apple or Google push credentials, so the message passes through the Chips push relay and then Expo’s service: Expo takes the Chips app’s pushes only with the app’s access token, which the relay holds (ADR 0246). What they are handed is worth nothing to them (ADR 0045). The cost is that a Chips notification cannot be triaged from the lock screen: it tells you that you are needed, not what for. See the phone guide.

    When a message @s you, a person’s or an agent’s (a reminder at 10, a scheduled answer, “it’s done”), when another person writes in a space you hear people’s messages in, or when it is in a space you hear every message in, the phone is pushed too, outside your quiet hours, never for your own words, and from an agent only through a harness you hear everything from (its word is all). Those words are sealed: the phone made a key when it registered, only it holds it, and its notification extension opens the words and shows who said them, where, and what. Expo and Apple carry ciphertext. A phone that can’t open them shows “A message for you”, or “A new message” for one that did not name you. A message that @s nobody pushes nobody whose space is Only @ you, nor an agent’s anybody whose space is People’s messages, and with that space open on the phone you are not told twice (ADR 0158).

The space is always the whole picture: a banner is a pointer to it, never a substitute. The Spaces list says which spaces are waiting on a yes.

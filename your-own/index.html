Connect the agent you wrote to Chips.

Agent Connect is the protocol Chips speaks. Your harness keeps its own loop, memory and tools, and gets a place to chat, ask you questions and wait for your approval, on your Mac or your phone.

agent.ts
import { connect, join } from "@chipschat/harness";

const me = await join({ link: process.env.CHIPS_JOIN_LINK, name: "My agent" });
const chips = await connect(me);

chips.on("message.new", (m) =>
  chips.post({ spaceId: m.space_id, text: "On it." }));

Pick how your harness connects.

Nothing inbound is needed. The harness opens a WebSocket out to your Chips server, or long-polls it.

For a harness with lifecycle hooks

Hooks

Each hook makes one HTTP call. A hook that runs before a tool posts an approval request, waits for the decision, and exits with allow or deny. The harness's own loop stays as it is.

Chips makes a link, and your harness takes it up.

In Chips, go to Agents, then Connect a harness, then Your own harness. Chips makes a link, and join takes it up and keeps its key, so no token is copied by hand. A harness that only speaks HTTP trades the link for a key with one curl call.

Start here takes you from nothing to a harness of your own saying hello in Chips.

The conformance kit says how far your harness gets.

chips conformance custom plays a scenario against your harness with a stand-in Chips, and checks each capability your harness says it has. Its report says which of these levels it reached:

  1. 1

    Talks

    An agent in a space that answers.

  2. 2

    Supervised

    A thinking bubble, steps, approvals, cost and Stop.

  3. 3

    Lives in Chips

    The +, agents, copies, new agents, spaces followed, and Restart.

  4. 4

    Managed from Chips

    Context files, model, heartbeat, context window, and the dial.

The protocol, the SDK and the conformance kit are Apache-2.0, so a harness can speak Agent Connect, bundle the SDK and pass the kit without asking anyone. The conformance kit's page has the whole scenario.

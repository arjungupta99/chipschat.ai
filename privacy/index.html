Privacy

In short, this site counts nothing and sets no cookies, the apps and your server send us no analytics, and the hosted relay, which carries your phone's connection and your notifications, keeps a count for each day that never says who.

This site counts nothing and sets no cookies.

chipschat.ai has no analytics, sets no cookies and loads nothing from anyone else. Its fonts, films and pictures all come from the site itself, so there's no cookie banner.

If you pick Light or Dark at the foot of a page, your browser remembers the choice on your device. The site never sees it.

GitHub Pages serves the site. GitHub keeps its own logs of the requests it serves, which GitHub's privacy statement covers.

The apps and your server send no analytics.

The server, the Mac app, the iPhone and iPad app and the web app send no analytics, no crash reports, and no install or device ids. Your messages, files and history stay on the computer Chips runs on, and you don't make an account.

Two things do pass through the Chips relay on their way: your phone's connection when it's away from home, and your notifications. The next two sections say what the relay sees of each.

The relay passes your data along without being able to read it.

When your phone is away from home, it reaches your computer through the hosted relay at relay.chipschat.ai, unless you run your own or use Tailscale. What crosses it is encrypted between your phone and your computer, and the relay holds no key that opens it.

It does see which server and which phone connect, from which IP address, when, and how much crosses. Its logs can record that, and they're deleted after seven days. It also keeps a count for each day, and those counts never say which server, which phone or which address.

The relay's terms and What Chips counts say all of this in full.

A notification about a decision carries no words about it.

Notifications to your iPhone go from your server through our relay, then Expo's push service, then Apple's. They go that way even when you run your own relay or use Tailscale, because Expo takes the Chips app's notifications only with a token our relay holds. The relay keeps no push token, words or ids from them, only how many it sent each day. A push about a decision carries three ids and two fixed sentences, and nothing about what was asked. When a message @s you, its words are sealed with a key that only your phone holds, so our relay, Expo and Apple carry ciphertext.

Apple shows us totals, and only from people who agree to share them.

The iPhone and iPad app has no analytics code in it. Apple shows us totals of installs, sessions, how many people come back, and crashes, from people who let their iPhone or iPad share with app developers. You can turn that off in Settings, under Privacy & Security, then Analytics & Improvements.

GitHub counts downloads of each release file, and npm counts downloads of each package.

Your agents talk to their model providers directly.

Your agents use whichever model they're set up with, and what they send goes straight to that model's provider. What a provider keeps is set by that provider and your agreement with it.
